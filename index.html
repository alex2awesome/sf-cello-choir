Founded byLucas and Alexander Spangher

Lucas Spangher

Cello

Lucas Spangher is a cellist actively performing in the Bay Area. Growing up, he studied cello performance at the Juilliard Pre-College and performed regularly in Carnegie Hall with the New York Youth Symphony, where he held first stand. He was a finalist in the 2008 Island Symphony Concerto Competition, held the second seat at New York All-State in 2009, and studied with Fred Raimi at Duke University.

He has organized and performed in numerous critically acclaimed concerts, including the massive 2022 Ukrainian Benefit Concert at Herbst Theatre in San Francisco. He plays a Prescott 5/4 cello from 1812, for which he is grateful to you all, the SF classical music community, for sponsoring through donations, and to Kolstein's Music of Manhattan for further sponsoring it through a grant.

He holds a Ph.D. in computer science from UC Berkeley and did a postdoc in plasma physics at MIT.

Alexander Spangher

Piano

Alexander has been playing piano since he was three years old. He was twice showcased in the New York State All-State piano recital, played in the New York State All-State Orchestra, performed for Off-Broadway plays in New York City, and attended the Juilliard School (for double bass). He has played in a number of critically acclaimed concerts, including the 2022 Ukraine Benefit Concert at Herbst Theatre with Svetlana Nikitenko (soprano, SF Opera).

He transcribes (for example the Carmen Suite for piano), composes (his work has been premiered at various venues), choreographs (with Sheila Paloma of the American Ballet Theatre), and does computational music research as a hobby. He also worked for the New York Times as a journalist and data scientist and is a postdoc in the Computer Science Department at Stanford.

Why we do this

We put on free classical concerts around the Bay Area. Admission is by donation, and the donations pay the musicians.

Good live music should be within everyone's reach, and the people who make it should be paid for their time. Our Dvořák program alone took each of the professionals on stage more than twenty hours of rehearsal and commuting. Donations are tax-deductible through our nonprofit partner, InterMusic SF.

There is an old story about the Bay Area. In 1899 the University of California held an international competition for the design of its Berkeley campus. The Parisian architect Émile Bénard won it, came out to see the site, and would not stay to build it. The job went to the fourth-place finisher, John Galen Howard. Accounts of why differ, but the version that has stuck is that Bénard could not imagine living somewhere with so little culture.

The Bay Area has spent a century proving him wrong. We would like to do our small part: music made here, by people who live here, for anyone who shows up on a Sunday evening.

MusiciansGuests, alumni and past members

Playing with us on November 29

Peggy Yin

Soprano

Peggy Yin is the first-place winner of the American Protégé International Competition, the Golden Classical Music Awards International Competition, and the National Collegiate Competition. She sang with the Ferris Choral Fellows at Harvard University and the Harvard New Music Ensemble, was named an All-Eastern vocalist and a New York All-State vocalist and flutist, has twice performed solo at Carnegie Hall, and has installed sound art at venues such as the MIT Museum. She grew up in New York, studying at the Manhattan School of Music and Juilliard, and later studied with Frank Kelley at Berklee College of Music.

Judah Laykin

Tenor and composer

Judah Laykin is a composer, vocalist and cellist based in Berkeley. He was a semi-finalist in the 2022 Petrichor International Composition Competition, the recipient of Harvard's 2023 George Arthur Knight Prize for compositional excellence, and the winner of the 2026 Aliénor International Composition Competition. Largely self-taught, he began composing at seventeen, developing a style rooted in his own mystical experiences and perfumed by the rich odeur of Impressionism. His music seeks to open doors into spaces of deep introspection, where themes of love, light and redemption are summoned into conscious awareness through the force of musical color.

Alumni and past members

Melissa Lam

Cello

Melissa Lam is the grand prize winner of the 2025 Antonín Dvořák Competition and has earned first prizes in the 2025 Pacific Musical Society & Foundation Competition, the 2024 American Virtuoso Competition, and the 2023 US Open Music Competition. She was also recently named a winner of the 2026 YoungArts Competition and a finalist for From the Top. Melissa recently premiered the cello ensemble works of Gautier Capuçon's newest album, Gaïa, alongside the renowned cellist at Davies Symphony Hall.

Elmer Carter III

Cello

Elmer Carter III is recognized for a resonant tone and an expressive, collaborative approach to music-making. A recipient of the Denis de Coteau Fellowship with the SF Ballet Orchestra, with degrees from the SF Conservatory of Music (Advanced Certificate and MM), he co-founded Mosaic Strings and is an active freelance artist. Recent engagements include section work with the SF Philharmonic and leadership of the Echo Trio through the SF Symphony's Adventures in Music program, along with the Gem Trio and Lora Quartet, as well as appearances alongside the SF Composers Chamber Orchestra, SF Symphony, and Fort Smith Symphony. He has contributed to Sony recording sessions and collaborated with Kronos Quartet.

Gabriel Beistline

Cello

Gabriel Beistline has toured with orchestras throughout the United States, Germany, Lithuania, Armenia, Russia and Japan. He has also enjoyed many experiences as a chamber musician, including a performance of Felix Mendelssohn's String Octet with violin virtuoso Cho-Liang Lin, James Dunham of the Cleveland Quartet, and Lynn Harrell, in a concert series commemorating Mendelssohn's 200th birthday. Gabriel holds a BM from the New England Conservatory under the instruction of Yeesun Kim, cellist of the Borromeo String Quartet, and a Master of Music from Rice University under the tutelage of Lynn Harrell.

Percy Liang

Piano

Percy Liang is a professor in the Stanford computer science department. Percy started studying piano at the age of eight, earned a minor in music from MIT, and has participated in various chamber music groups, music festivals, and competitions. In 2008 he won the over-21 division of KDFC's Classical Star Search. He has also performed with the MIT Symphony (2004) after winning the MIT Concerto Competition and with the Phoenix Symphony (2000) after winning the Phoenix Young Musicians Competition.

SupportKeep the concerts free

Every dollar goes to the musicians. For each program we try to pay the professionals on stage for their rehearsal time as well as the performance.

Donations are tax-deductible through our nonprofit partner, InterMusic SF. Admission is always free, and seats fill, so RSVP early and give your spot back if your plans change.

Cellists and pianists in the Bay Area who would like to play on a future program are welcome to get in touch.
