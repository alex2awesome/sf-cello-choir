Lucas Spangher is a cellist actively performing in the Bay Area. Growing up, he studied cello performance at the Juilliard Pre-College and performed regularly in Carnegie Hall with the New York Youth Symphony. He held the second seat at New York All-State in 2009 and studied with Fred Raimi at Duke University.

He has organized and performed in numerous critically acclaimed concerts, including the massive 2022 Ukrainian Benefit Concert at Herbst Theatre in San Francisco. He plays a Prescott 5/4 cello from 1812, for which he is grateful to you all, the SF classical music community, for sponsoring through donations, and to Kolstein's Music of Manhattan for further sponsoring it through a grant.

He holds a Ph.D. in computer science from UC Berkeley and did a postdoc in plasma physics at MIT.

Why we do thisOur purpose

We put on free classical concerts around the Bay Area. Admission is by donation, and the donations pay the professional musicians who play with us.

Good live music should be within everyone's reach, and the people who make it should be paid for their time. Our Dvořák program alone cost each of the professionals on stage more than twenty hours of rehearsal and commuting before a single note was played in public. Donations are tax-deductible through our nonprofit partner, InterMusic SF.

There is an old story about this place. In 1899 the University of California held an international competition for the design of its Berkeley campus. The Parisian architect Émile Bénard won it, came out to see the site, and would not stay to build it. The job went to the fourth-place finisher, John Galen Howard. Accounts of why differ, but the version that has stuck is that Bénard could not imagine living somewhere with so little culture.

The Bay Area has spent a century proving him wrong, and we would like to do our small part. Music made here, by people who live here, for neighbors who show up on a Sunday evening, is part of what turns a place into a community.

Alumni and past membersMusicians who have played with us

Melissa Lam

Cello

Melissa Lam is the grand prize winner of the 2025 Antonín Dvořák Competition and has earned first prizes in the 2025 Pacific Musical Society & Foundation Competition, the 2024 American Virtuoso Competition, and the 2023 US Open Music Competition. She was also recently named a winner of the 2026 YoungArts Competition and a finalist for From the Top. Melissa recently premiered the cello ensemble works of Gautier Capuçon's newest album, Gaïa, alongside the renowned cellist at Davies Symphony Hall.

Elmer Carter III

Cello

Elmer Carter III is recognized for a resonant tone and an expressive, collaborative approach to music-making. A recipient of the Denis de Coteau Fellowship with the SF Ballet Orchestra, with degrees from the SF Conservatory of Music (Advanced Certificate and MM), he co-founded Mosaic Strings and is an active freelance artist. Recent engagements include section work with the SF Philharmonic and leadership of the Echo Trio through the SF Symphony's Adventures in Music program, along with the Gem Trio and Lora Quartet, as well as appearances alongside the SF Composers Chamber Orchestra, SF Symphony, and Fort Smith Symphony. He has contributed to Sony recording sessions and collaborated with Kronos Quartet.

Gabriel Beistline

Cello

Gabriel Beistline has toured with orchestras throughout the United States, Germany, Lithuania, Armenia, Russia and Japan. He has also enjoyed many experiences as a chamber musician, including a performance of Felix Mendelssohn's String Octet with violin virtuoso Cho-Liang Lin, James Dunham of the Cleveland Quartet, and Lynn Harrell, in a concert series commemorating Mendelssohn's 200th birthday. Gabriel holds a BM from the New England Conservatory under the instruction of Yeesun Kim, cellist of the Borromeo String Quartet, and a Master of Music from Rice University under the tutelage of Lynn Harrell.

Percy Liang

Piano

Percy Liang is a professor in the Stanford computer science department. Percy started studying piano at the age of eight, earned a minor in music from MIT, and has participated in various chamber music groups, music festivals, and competitions. In 2008 he won the over-21 division of KDFC's Classical Star Search. He has also performed with the MIT Symphony (2004) after winning the MIT Concerto Competition and with the Phoenix Symphony (2000) after winning the Phoenix Young Musicians Competition.

Alex Spangher

Piano

Alex has been playing piano since he was three years old. He was twice showcased in the New York State All-State piano recital, played in the New York State All-State Orchestra, performed for Off-Broadway plays in New York City, and attended conservatory (for double bass). He has played in a number of critically acclaimed concerts, including the 2022 Ukraine Benefit Concert at Herbst Theatre with Svetlana Nikitenko (soprano, SF Opera). He transcribes (for example the Carmen Suite for piano), composes (his work has been premiered at various venues), choreographs (with Sheila Paloma of the American Ballet Theatre), and does computational music research as a hobby. He also worked for the New York Times as a journalist and data scientist and is a postdoc in the Computer Science Department at Stanford.
